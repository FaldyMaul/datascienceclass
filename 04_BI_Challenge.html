the companyWhat is Arunika Fiber?

Arunika Fiber sells home internet over fiber-optic cable in 12 Indonesian cities. Customers pay a monthly fee for a speed package (30 to 300 Mbps). On top of that they can add Arunika TV (10 basic channels plus four paid channel packs), a one-week Speed Booster, a Wi-Fi extender or a static IP address. They manage everything in the MyArunika app, where they also collect loyalty points called Arunika Poin.

The business is healthy but growth is slowing. The network reaches only part of each city, one in six bills is paid late, and the customer survey shows more unhappy customers than happy ones. Management wants decisions backed by data, not opinions.

Arunika Fiber is fictional. Every number was generated for this class, so nothing here describes a real company or real people. The patterns are realistic on purpose: there is something to find behind every question.

    how to workSix steps: the CRISP-DM path

    The same six steps used in the churn case. The data is already clean, so step 3 is short. Spend your time on steps 1, 4 and 5.

    1. Business understanding

      Write the decision in one sentence: “Management must decide … by choosing between …”. Name the one number that tells you whether the decision worked. Then write your hypothesis: “I think … because …”.

    2. Data understanding

      Read the data dictionary and the timeline. For each sheet ask: what is one row? A customer, a month, a sale? Note which sheets hold the evidence for your hypothesis.

    3. Data preparation

      Nothing to clean. Connect the sheets you need, check that dates are dates and Rupiah fields are currency, and create the few calculated fields your question needs, such as a late-payment rate or cost per watched hour.

    4. Analysis modelling

      Test the hypothesis with comparisons: by segment, by city, by month, before and after an event. Look for the group that behaves differently from the rest. If the first hypothesis fails, that is a result too. Write the next one.

    5. Evaluation

      Would a sceptical manager believe it? Check the size of each group, rule out another explanation, and put a number on the opportunity: how many customers, how many Rupiah.

    6. Deployment

      Build one page that someone can read in a minute: a headline that states the finding, three to five charts as evidence, and a recommendation with the next action. Share the link.

    From sheet to dashboard

    1. Download the workbook, open sheets.new, then File → Import → Upload and choose “Replace spreadsheet”.
    2. Open datastudio.google.com and create a Blank report.
    3. Choose the Google Sheets connector, pick the spreadsheet, then one worksheet. Keep “Use first row as headers” ticked. Click Add.
    4. Repeat Add data for every sheet you need. Each sheet becomes its own data source.
    5. Start with customer_360: one row per customer, already summarised.

    Three things that save time

    1. Rates need a formula. Averaging a percentage column gives the wrong answer when groups differ in size. Create a calculated field, for example SUM(late_or_unpaid_bills) / SUM(bills).
    2. Most sheets already carry city, segment and package_name, so you rarely need to blend two sheets.
    3. For a map, set city to type Geo → City, or use geo_point in the areas sheet as Geo → Latitude, Longitude.

    before you shareWhat a good dashboard looks like

    • The title is the finding, not the topic. “Eight areas hold most of the unmet demand” beats “Fiber dashboard”.
    • Three to five charts, each answering one question. Delete any chart you cannot explain in one sentence.
    • Compare, do not just count. Every number sits next to a benchmark: another segment, last month, the company average.
    • One or two filters (city, segment or month) so the reader can check their own area.
    • It ends with a decision: what to do, for whom, and how big the effect could be in customers or Rupiah.
    • It is honest about limits: what the data cannot tell you and what you would test first.
